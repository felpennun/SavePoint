\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

SavePoint is a web platform for cataloguing video games, managing a personal collection and acting as a reproducible testbed for recommender systems. The author designed and implemented the product with a catalogue, library, physical and digital copies, ratings, lists, comments, profiles, privacy, social relations, recommendations and a research dashboard. The application supports the work, while the dissertation focuses on reproducible recommendation research.

The system compares sixteen reference algorithms: baselines, content-based variants, neighbourhood collaborative filtering, and hybrid approaches with diversity re-ranking. The dissertation describes item and user representations, tag weighting, facet similarity, external quality, popularity, negative evidence, cold start, and hybrid combination. The offline protocol fixes corpus, snapshots, seeds, shared candidates, exclusions and ranking metrics at $K\in\{5,10,20\}$.

The published v15 evidence uses 400 active synthetic users and 79 evaluable users, with a single test run. It supports an internal comparison under the frozen corpus and protocol, but it does not represent real users or justify automatic generalisation. The dissertation explicitly separates that publication from the later implementation contract.

The author applied an agent-assisted engineering methodology governed through GSD and conceptually organised with Obsidian. Tools were used under the author's direction, review and final decisions. Felipe Peña Núñez is the sole author and remains responsible for analysis, design, implementation and validation.

\vspace{.5cm}
\textbf{Keywords:} video games, recommender systems, offline evaluation, reproducibility, collaborative filtering, content-based recommendation, data provenance.
