\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

SavePoint is a web application for cataloguing a personal video game collection, inspired by
Goodreads and Letterboxd, that also serves as the academic platform for this final degree
project. The project has a twofold goal. It gives a user an orderly way to record played and
pending games, rate them, inventory physical and digital copies together with their
provenance, and expose a public profile. It also builds a reproducible testbed on which to
implement and compare content based, collaborative filtering and hybrid recommendation
algorithms, using synthetic users, explainable results and documented data provenance.

This document describes the system that was built. SavePoint serves a real scale catalogue
of more than three hundred thousand works imported from the IGDB database through a resumable
importer, alongside a curated corpus of one hundred and fifty games taken from Wikidata under
a public domain dedication that acts as a citable reference. On top of that catalogue a user
keeps a backlog, rates games, inventories physical and digital copies together with their
format and edition, and publishes a public profile. The interface, with light and dark
themes and verified for accessibility, sorts and filters the catalogue by its metadata and
offers two deterministic recommenders: a popularity baseline and a genre affinity page
driven by each user's own recorded activity. The system starts reproducibly in a documented local environment and runs
without any connection to external services.

The work pays particular attention to two aspects. The first is evidentiary rigour: every
architectural decision is recorded in a decision document, goal verification is carried out
independently, each result carries an author acceptance sign off, and the figures reported
here come from that evidence rather than from estimates. The second is the methodology of
engineering assisted by artificial intelligence agents, governed through a specification
driven development method, applied and evaluated across the whole project and versioned in
the repository itself, so that the engineering process is as inspectable and reproducible by
third parties as the product. The systematic comparison of recommenders is presented as
future work that this reproducible testbed enables, not as an achieved result.

\textbf{Keywords:} video game cataloguing, recommender systems, reproducible testbed, data
provenance, agent assisted software engineering, specification driven development, Django,
Next.js.
