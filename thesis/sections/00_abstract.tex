\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

SavePoint is a web application for cataloguing a personal video game collection, inspired by
Goodreads and Letterboxd, that also serves as the academic platform for this final degree
project. The project has a twofold goal. It gives a user an orderly way to record played and
pending games, rate them, inventory physical and digital copies together with their
provenance, and expose a public profile. It also builds a reproducible testbed on which
later phases will implement and compare content based, collaborative filtering and hybrid
recommendation algorithms, using synthetic users, explainable results and documented data
provenance.

This document describes the first delivery of the project, covering two phases that are
already closed and verified. The first phase built a complete vertical slice, deployed on
the internet at zero cost, with a lawful catalogue of one hundred and fifty games taken from
Wikidata under a public domain dedication. The second phase grew that catalogue to more than
three hundred thousand works imported from the IGDB database through a resumable importer, it
redesigned the interface with a product grade intent, and it added catalogue sorting and
filtering, several simulated demonstration accounts and a genre affinity recommendations
page driven by each user's own recorded activity.

The work pays particular attention to two aspects. The first is evidentiary rigour: every
architectural decision is recorded in a decision document, every phase has an independent
goal verification and an author acceptance sign off, and the figures reported here come from
that evidence rather than from estimates. The second is the methodology of engineering
assisted by artificial intelligence agents, governed through a specification driven
development method, applied phase by phase and versioned in the repository itself, so that
the engineering process is as inspectable and reproducible by third parties as the product.
The recommender comparison work is presented as planned future work, not as an achieved
result.

\textbf{Keywords:} video game cataloguing, recommender systems, reproducible testbed, data
provenance, agent assisted software engineering, specification driven development, Django,
Next.js.
